# Governance checklists per architecture (lead, Fable 5.1, 2026-10-21)

Per D4.0 (3)(d): a governance checklist per architecture listing every key class, its holder, k-of-n, timelock and appeal route. Key classes follow the R24 reviewer's three groups (`reviews/dao-skeptic-r24.md` X-3): on-chain authorities, off-chain trust roots (A11), meta-authorities; plus the OS-local trust roots that A1 and A2 add. The insider rule applies to every row meant to resist custodian capture: insider keys with approval power at most k-1; the appeal or veto route never needs the quorum that approved; every key has a published affiliation (TM-R24-2). Every row is designed, not yet tested; the Status column names the acceptance test (`architecture/acceptance-backlog.md`) that would change it. "n/a" means the class has no sensible k-of-n or timelock on a single-user desktop and says why.

## 1. A1 and A2: OS-local trust roots (one user, one machine)

On a single-user desktop the holder is the user, through the bridge (A1) or the authority core (A2). k-of-n and timelocks make no sense for most rows; the governance questions are custody, rotation, what a revocation does, and what the user can override. The A2 column says what changes when the core and broker run as separate processes or a separate OS account.

## 4. Cross-checks the article applies to all three tables

1. Every row names a holder, a k-of-n (or says why n/a), a delay (or says why none), an appeal route and a log; a row without an appeal route is a capture point.
2. No row lets a DAO key mint runtime authority (I2), and no OS row depends on DAO state (I1).
3. Every revocation record carries its reason class (security or policy) and is written to the R30 ledger before any derived artefact (status bit, tombstone, burn) is produced (C-4).
4. The witness row is today the only row that meets the insider rule by construction; every other anti-capture row meets it only as designed.
