# Gate 2 report: systems that already solve the problems

DRAFT SKELETON, 2026-10-20. Written by the lead (Claude Fable 5.1) for a reader who did not watch the run. Sections marked [pending] are filled after the final merge and verification. Gate 2 runs autonomously by the user's decision D1.5: the shortlist below is the lead's decision and Stage 3 starts without a pause; any sandboxed execution of third-party code listed in section 7 stays unexecuted unless the user approves that specific run.

## 1. Outcome

Stage 2 found maintained, licence-compatible open-source candidates for every one of the 30 requirements (586 unique codebases from 1,147 source records, 185 of them candidates, no requirement with fewer than four), but the four properties the Alpha most needs do not exist anywhere as working code: an action gate that refuses a privileged route when no approval record exists (R11), a host-verifiable human review step for knowledge promotion (R14), rollback of a desktop service to a hashed known-good baseline (R04), and per-right personhood assurance with published error rates (R25). Stage 3 therefore inspects fifteen codebases as components to build those properties around, not as drop-in replacements, and the architecture decision will turn on how thin the custom layer can be.

## 2. Method (how the longlist was built)

Stage 2 ran in four waves after Gate 1 approved the 30 requirements unchanged (D1.1):

1. Term banks for Chinese, French, German and Dutch (`glossary/`), each verifying terms in real technical use and rejecting dictionary translations. Cross-language result: practitioners keep the English word for most agent, capability and DAO vocabulary; native terms with real use cluster around auditing, authorization, containment and durable execution.
2. Eight capability-branch agents (A-H) searched the global platforms with English and term-bank strings, each owning three to five requirements and running a disconfirmation task on its strongest candidates. A Japanese/Korean probe applied the master prompt's tripwire; it did not fire (D2.1), so no Japanese or Korean language agent ran, and the blind spots (Velog, Naver D2, Tistory, Note.com) are recorded.
3. Four language agents searched the regional platforms and ran native terms on GitHub, verifying canonical copies of mirrored projects and reading non-English documentation.
4. A provenance agent merged every source record into unique codebases (`codebases.jsonl`) and built the coverage matrix; the lead sent targeted second passes to the branch agents for thin cells, platforms never searched and disagreements between agents; a fresh-context verifier sampled 10 percent of rows.

Every row is in `ledger/discovery-<agent>.jsonl` with its query, date, evidence level and disposition; every empty or blocked search is in `ledger/negative-<agent>.jsonl`. Nothing was cloned or executed in Stage 2; "inspected" means read through the platform's API or web pages.

## 3. Shortlist criteria (set before the results were read)

A codebase goes to Stage 3 deep inspection when it meets all of the first three and at least one of the last two:

1. It addresses a P0 requirement (R01, R02, R03, R10, R11, R12, R14, R18, R19, R22, R23, R26, R29, R30), or it addresses two or more P1 requirements at once.
2. Its record is `inspected` at the manifest or source level, with a licence read from the licence file, and a disconfirmation attempt is recorded (abandonment, advisories, open core, complexity, compromise).
3. It is plausible on the Alpha's constraints (`gaps.md` standing constraints): runs locally on the three desktop OSes or states its per-OS matrix honestly; no mandatory cloud or telemetry; licence compatible with MIT or flagged for legal review rather than silently accepted.
4. It demonstrates, with a test, audit or reproducible example, at least one property from the threat model's "must demonstrate" table (`threat-model-draft.md` section 4), or
5. It is the strongest available option for a requirement where nothing demonstrates the property yet, in which case the dossier's job is to measure the distance.

Secondary ordering, used to cut from 20-35 suitable to 8-15: breadth of threats closed (T1-T20), reusability as a library rather than an application, maintenance signals (commit recency, release cadence, maintainer organisation), and whether a less obvious or non-English candidate adds a design the mainstream options lack. Popularity is not a criterion. Three or four of the shortlisted codebases, the most security- or architecture-critical, go to Fable 5.1 inspectors; the rest to Opus 5.5 (master prompt 6.1).

Exclusions applied before scoring: EVM-only governance or credential systems are patterns, not candidates, for the Solana-targeted DAO (recorded as `lead` with `chain: evm`); hosted-only or server-only products are patterns for R10 and R11; demos younger than a month are leads unless they are the only implementation of a needed design.

## 4. What was established (with confidence)

Counts (high confidence; produced by the merge script from validated ledgers, FINAL v2): 1,147 source records and 1,298 recorded empty or blocked searches from 13 agents; 586 unique codebases after deduplication, of which 185 are candidates (inspected manifest or source, licence read from a file, disconfirmation attempted), 278 are leads, 104 excluded with reasons and 18 exist only as the target of a counterexample. Pass 2 added 45 candidates (21 through the language agents, 24 through the branches) and closed every requirement that had fewer than four candidates after pass 1.

By area, what the longlist shows (confidence medium-high; each statement rests on inspected rows and is checked again in Stage 3):

- Authorization (R01). Object-capability tokens with attenuation and expiry exist as maintained, Node-embeddable code (Biscuit under the Eclipse Foundation; Cedar as a policy engine with a WASM build). Mid-session revocation with a test exists only in two young projects (Aegis, Attenuated-Delegation-Chain). The two halves have not been combined by anyone found.
- Containment (R02). Linux and macOS confinement is solved many times over (bubblewrap, Landlock and seccomp wrappers, seatbelt profiles, microVMs). Windows without administrator rights is the open problem: one TypeScript candidate claims it (Convira), the best-known one needs an elevated install (sandbox-runtime), and a measurement shows restricted-token sandboxes break piped child processes. Fresh advisories against three sandboxes (microsandbox, nono, sandbox-runtime) show this layer is where attackers look.
- Secret brokering (R03). Credential proxies that keep keys out of child environments exist (Aegis, Greyproxy, Terok's phantom tokens, oidc-agent's agent model); the vercel/ai harness advisory that exposed host credentials to sandboxed code is a reminder of why (T2).
- Durable execution and evidence (R10-R12). Embedded durable execution exists in Go with SQLite (Durable Task) and in TypeScript (OpenWorkflow); the hosted products (Temporal's server, Restate, Inngest, DBOS on Postgres) are patterns for a single-user desktop. Append-only Merkle logs exist in Node (hypercore) and as the transparency-log standard (Tessera, rekor-tiles), but no candidate anywhere refuses an action for lack of an approval record: that gate must be built. Selective-disclosure credentials have an active TypeScript implementation (identity-common-ts, SD-JWT VC) and a mature Go one (irmago); C2PA and in-toto supply attribution schemas; human-versus-AI attribution carried from the point of work exists in no format as a first-class field.
- Knowledge and local data (R13-R17). Versioned, provenance-tracked file data is mature in science (DataLad); no knowledge or memory system keeps source text separate from AI derivations behind a verifiable review (Mem0 keeps no source messages; Graphiti has an injection advisory and no embedded backend). End-to-end encrypted local-first sync with real access control is research-grade (Keyhive, Willow) or single-maintainer (Evolu). Vault primitives are solid (age, libsodium, OS keychains, audited Shamir); offline retrieval is solved (FTS5, sqlite-vec, Orama).
- Browser and add-ons (R18-R21). Signed, hash-pinned, revocable manifests with offline verification exist as tuf-js and pass the TUF conformance suite. Browser-agent gating lives, in every candidate, in the extension or page (Nanobrowser's service-worker classifier is the best of these); the academic systems with measured results (Intact 12.8 percent attack success versus 73.1 percent; "Stop Means Stop" finding approval gates leak sibling effects in five of six frameworks) show what a host-side gate must achieve. Extension-to-native channels have well-documented reference designs (KeePassXC, Bitwarden, Tailscale's LocalAPI with Origin and Host rejection), each GPL or large, so they are patterns to learn.
- DAO credentials and governance (R22-R25). On Solana, non-transferable credentials with issuer revocation exist as audited mainnet code (solana-foundation/rewards on Token-2022); attestation (SAS) and governance (Realms, Squads) exist but with gaps the dossiers must weigh (SAS unaudited and its deployed binary behind source; Squads AGPL-3.0 and immutable; Realms' upstream archived). Personhood systems publish no false-reject rates; agent-farming limits exist in none.
- Contribution accounting (R26-R28). The mainstream tools are dead or stalled (SourceCred wound down 2022 and warns against its own algorithm at low trust, Praise and Coordinape stale, MACI archived 2026-07); the live material is schemas (hypercerts-lexicon, CRediT roles) and research code (MeritRank, EigenTrust, conference reviewer assignment). Documented farming (tea.xyz, 150,000 packages) and the finding that collusion rings are mostly undetectable from bids are the strongest evidence in this area.
- Contrarian (R29, R30). The most feature-complete thin-layer candidate (OpenClaw) carries 722 published advisories and disclaims multi-tenant isolation; the foundation-governed one (Goose) is MCP-native and the serious comparison. For an off-chain Phase 1, witnessed transparency logs (Tessera plus tlog-witness) and cooperative-run membership and voting tools (Loomio, Belenios) cover membership, claims and advisory votes without tokens; what a chain adds is public anchoring of commitments, which Token-2022 and SPL Memo patterns can supply later.

Non-English and less obvious finds that matter: Terok (credential vault handing containers short-lived tokens, found only through the Helmholtz research-software index), the German EUDI relying-party-registrar (an RFC 6962 Merkle log with a WebCrypto verifier in TypeScript, from a federal ministry's open-source group), Greywall and Greyproxy (found through a LinuxFr article), Belenios (Inria's verifiable voting), Yivi irmago (Dutch privacy-by-design credentials with status lists), and DataLad and Research Object Crate from scientific data management, which solve the provenance-and-promotion problem without calling it AI memory.

Counterexample findings that remove or downgrade mainstream options (144 counterexample rows after pass 1, 59 more from the sweep of 127 candidates): LiteLLM's PyPI compromise (March 2026) and its enterprise-directory licence; Open WebUI's branding clause; Continue read-only and Flowise archived; Mastra's open-core directories; MCP TypeScript SDK host-header validation off by default (CVE-2025-66414); Playwright MCP describing itself as "not a security boundary"; browser-use CVE-2025-47241; sigstore-js with four advisories in 2026 and no rollback concept; Tantivy's code-execution advisory (2026-10-16); OpenAttestation and sd-jwt-js archived; c2pa-node's release missing its Windows binary; cc-haha relicensed to non-commercial on 2026-10-15; openreview-matcher requiring a proprietary solver with no licence file; gov4git abandoned since 2024-05.

## 5. Coverage and blind spots

Full tables are in `ledger/coverage-matrix.md` (FINAL, 13 ledger pairs: 1,099 discovery rows, 1,297 negative rows). The short version:

- Every platform in the master prompt's lists was searched at least once; the five that no pass-1 agent touched (SourceForge, ACL Anthology, Maven Central, NuGet, Semantic Scholar) were covered in pass 2 and yielded little: SourceForge holds stale snapshots and name collisions, Maven's index lags GitHub and its search timed out on most calls, NuGet produced one new R10 lead (the Durable Task engine) and the Windows AppContainer libraries, ACL Anthology's search is client-rendered and unusable by API, and Semantic Scholar rate-limits anonymous callers. GitHub carried the load: 1,274 purposeful searches in seven languages, 425 unique codebases, 160 candidates. npm was the second source of candidates (31).
- Platforms that stayed closed to anonymous research, recorded as blocked rather than searched: Gitee repository search (lookup by name works, so Chinese upstreams were resolved from GitHub and GitCode hits), GitCode's API (401), OpenI (agreement gate, not bypassed per the respect-platforms rule), CNKI (host unreachable; the overseas mirror returns nothing). The arXiv API returned 429 for every agent all day (shared egress); OpenAlex and Hugging Face paper pages substituted, and OpenAlex itself began returning a shared daily-budget notice in pass 2. These are the research's documented blind spots: Gitee-only and OpenI-only Chinese code, and paper-to-code chains that only arXiv's own API exposes.
- Language x requirement: after pass 2 no cell is unexplored for English, Chinese, French or German; Dutch has one (R20). Japanese and Korean have 22 and 21 unexplored cells by decision D2.1 (the probe's tripwire did not fire; reversal condition recorded). Regional platforms searched in native terms: openCode.de 109 searches (15 codebases, the German public-sector work on audit logs, provenance and EUDI wallets), HAL 122 (8 codebases, none a candidate), Framagit 94, code.gouv.fr 63 (data file dated 2024-09-18), university GitLabs 108 (German, French, Dutch), RSD 49, developer.overheid.nl 50, 4TU 23.
- Non-English yield: 27 candidate codebases came from the four language agents (zh 11, fr 8, de 3, nl 5), of which the ones absent from every English branch are Terok's credential vault (R03, via Helmholtz RSD), the German EUDI relying-party-registrar Merkle log (R11), Greywall and Greyproxy (R02/R03, via LinuxFr), Belenios verifiable voting (R30), Yivi irmago status lists (R12/R23), opensider's native-messaging design (R20) and openEuler's Git governance (R30). Where practitioners keep the English word (most agent and DAO vocabulary), native-language search added mirrors and design documents rather than new codebases; where native terms exist (auditing, authorisation, containment), it added the codebases above.
- Thinnest requirements after dedup, each with four candidates: R13 (crash-consistent storage), R22 (Solana credentials), R24 (Solana governance), R25 (personhood), R27 (collusion resistance).

## 8. What is unresolved

- Whether any child process can be confined on Windows without administrator rights from a Node parent. The two candidates make opposite claims (sandbox-runtime needs a one-time elevated install; Convira says AppContainer plus Job Objects without admin) and a measurement repo shows restricted-token sandboxes cannot launch native children with piped stdio. Static reading may not settle this; it is sandboxed-execution request (a).
- Whether the approval-gated route (R11) and the host-verifiable promotion review (R14) should be one mechanism. No candidate implements either; the closest designs (CATP's "block if the audit append fails", Radicle's signed reviews, signed Git commits against an allowed-signers list) point in the same direction.
- Eight licence disagreements between agents remain in `prov-validation.md`; five are wording differences (Wasmtime's LLVM exception, KeePassXC-Browser's GPL version, SQLite's dedication, Bitwarden's split, wangfenjin/simple's dual licence) and three need the dossier's reading (f13 orchestrator's REUSE layout, cc-haha's relicensing date, gov4git's dual text). Five codebases have a status spread (cc-haha, HDP, askalf-browser-bridge, mute, openEuler).
- Audit reports for the proposed R15 vault stack (age/typage, keyring-node) were not found; only Privy's Shamir library has published audits.
- On-chain facts were read from public RPC on 2026-10-20 and can change: SAS's deployed binary predates its 2.0.0 source; Squads v4 is immutable on mainnet; Realms' upgrade authority is a DAO-governed PDA; Light Protocol sits under a 2-of-5 Squads vault without timelock.
- The DAO's own design documents stay status F (D1.4); every DAO-side candidate is judged against the whitepaper text alone, and the request to republish them is held with the other maintainer items (OI-1..OI-4).
- Branch H's "clean-check" rows cover abandonment, advisories, open core and complexity well, but documented compromises and incentive attacks (check e) only partly; Stage 3 reviewers should treat "clean" as "nothing found", not "nothing there".
